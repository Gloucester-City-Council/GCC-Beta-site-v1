Annual leave and bank holiday entitlement

Part-time staff, joiners and leavers each need an entitlement worked out from their own hours and dates, and bank holidays land on Mondays and Fridays far more often than on other days. This calculator works out annual leave and bank holiday entitlement in hours for the time someone is employed, then shows what is left to take.

How does your HR policy count bank holidays for a part year?

Annual leave and bank holidays are rounded separately, then added together. Statutory leave cannot be rounded down, and in the first year of employment part days must be rounded up to the nearest half day. A half day is half the full-time hours in a day.

    The calculation

    Bank holidays are the number of bank holidays × full-time hours in a day × FTE. Annual leave is the full-time annual leave days × full-time hours in a day × FTE. FTE is the contracted hours divided by the full-time hours. The calculation uses the exact FTE and rounds only at the end.

    Joiners and leavers

    Annual leave is the full-year figure multiplied by the days employed and divided by the days in the leave year. Bank holidays have two common approaches. One counts the bank holidays that fall inside the employment dates. The other scales the full-year bank holiday entitlement by the time employed. Choose the one your HR policy uses. The result shows the other figure too, so you can see the difference.

    Changes during the year

    When contracted hours or annual leave change on a date, the calculation splits the leave year at that date and works out each part with its own hours and days. Each bank holiday is valued at the FTE on the day it falls.

    When a bank holiday arrives

    Where entitlement works as an hours allowance, an employee books the hours they would normally have worked if the bank holiday falls on a working day. If it falls on a day off, they book nothing. Whatever is left stays in their allowance, subject to the council’s leave rules. Choose “Hours on each day” to see this for a working pattern.

    Why you choose the bank holidays

    Statutory minimum

    Workers have a right to 5.6 weeks of paid leave a year, capped at 28 days, and employers can count bank holidays towards it. For part-time workers with regular hours it is 5.6 times their weekly hours. The calculator compares the entitlement with that figure for the time employed. Part-time staff must not be treated less favourably than full-time staff, so contractual leave and bank holidays are given pro rata.

    Part days cannot be rounded down. In the first year of employment they must be rounded up to the nearest half day. GOV.UK builds up first-year leave one month at a time, so its figure for a joiner can differ slightly from the calendar days used here. Check statutory figures for joiners with the GOV.UK calculator. This check is a guide and not legal advice.

    What this tool does not decide

    It calculates entitlement for people on regular hours. It does not cover irregular hours or part-year workers, who build up leave from the hours they work. It does not set Gloucester City Council’s contractual or HR rules. Rounding, leave during sickness or family leave, unpaid absence, carry-over limits and how a leave year that starts or ends part way through employment is treated are for the HR policy and the employee’s terms to settle.
