Part-time bank holiday entitlement

Bank holidays land on Mondays and Fridays far more often than on other days, so counting only the ones an employee misses treats part-time staff unevenly. This works out a pro-rata entitlement in hours first, then shows where each bank holiday falls in their week.

How the calculation works

Why the entitlement comes first

The calculation

Number of bank holidays × full-time hours in a day × FTE. FTE is the contracted hours divided by the full-time hours. The calculation uses the exact FTE and rounds only the answer, to two decimal places.

When a bank holiday arrives

Where entitlement works as an hours allowance, an employee books the hours they would normally have worked if the bank holiday falls on a working day. If it falls on a day off, they book nothing. Whatever is left stays in their allowance, subject to the council’s leave rules. Choose “Hours on each day” to see this for a working pattern.

Why you choose the bank holidays

What this tool does not decide

It calculates pro-rata entitlement. It does not set Gloucester City Council’s contractual or HR rules. Recording, rounding, remaining hours, joiners and leavers, and changes to contracted hours are for the HR policy and the employee’s terms to settle.

Based on GOV.UK holiday entitlement guidance and ACAS bank holiday guidance. Dates come from the GOV.UK bank holiday calendar for England and Wales, checked on 26 November 2026.
