Money off your bill

Discounts based on who lives in the property, help if you're on a low income, and exemptions or premiums that depend on the property itself.

Discounts for your household

Single Person Discount — 25%

If you're the only adult (18 or over) living in the property, you can apply for a 25% discount. Keep paying your existing bill until you hear back.

Tell the council within 21 days if anything changes — someone moves in, or someone in the house turns 18 — or you may receive a penalty charge.

How to apply: online through a linked My Gloucester account, by emailing the completed application form to revenues@gloucester.gov.uk (include your Council Tax account reference in the subject line), or by post to Gloucester City Council, Revenues & Benefits, Eastgate Management Suite, Eastgate Street, Gloucester, GL1 1PA.

Students and student nurses

Full-time students and student nurses aren't counted when working out Council Tax. If everyone in a property is a student or student nurse, the property may be exempt from paying altogether; if only some people are, the household may get a discount.

Who counts as a full-time student
Someone studying at a "prescribed place of education" (established universities and most colleges), where either: they're under 20 and their course leads to a qualification up to (not above) A level standard, lasts more than 3 months and involves more than 12 hours of study a week; or their university/college course lasts at least a full academic or calendar year, requires at least 24 weeks' attendance a year, and involves at least 21 hours of study a week during term time. Distance learning, evening classes, and job-related day-release courses don't count.
Who counts as a student nurse
Nursing or midwifery students, including pre-registration undergraduates and post-registration health visitor trainees, on a course leading to registration under the Nurses, Midwives and Health Visitors Act 1997. Student nurses on traditional hospital-based training do not count as students.

How to apply: each student needs a student certificate from their place of study's bursar. Upload it with the application form online (My Gloucester), or email/post it in the same way as the Single Person Discount above.

Severely mentally impaired (SMI)

Someone who is severely mentally impaired isn't counted for Council Tax purposes — this covers conditions such as Alzheimer's and severe learning difficulties, where there's a severe and apparently permanent impairment of intelligence and social functioning.

To qualify, the person must be receiving one of: Armed Forces Independence Payment, Attendance Allowance, Disability Living Allowance (care component, high or middle rate), Disablement Pension with constant attendance allowance, Employment and Support Allowance, Incapacity Benefit, Income Support with disability premium, Personal Independence Payment (daily living component), Severe Disablement Allowance, Unemployability Supplement or Allowance, or the disability element of Working Tax Credit.

You'll also need a free certificate from a registered medical practitioner, and proof of the qualifying benefit. If everyone in the property is severely mentally impaired, the property may be fully exempt.

How to apply: as above — online, email (revenues@gloucester.gov.uk), or post.

Disabled reduction

If a property has been adapted for a disabled person (adult or child) who lives there as their main home, the bill is charged at one Council Tax band lower — for example, a Band C property is billed as Band B. Properties already in Band A can still get a reduction.

The property needs one of: a room (other than a bathroom, kitchen or toilet) used by the disabled person for a special reason, such as dialysis equipment; an extra bathroom or kitchen for their use; or extra floor space to allow indoor wheelchair use (outdoor-only wheelchairs don't qualify).

How to apply: as above — online, email, or post.

Council Tax Support — help if you're on a low income

Council Tax Support helps people on a low income pay their bill. The scheme is set locally for working-age people, and nationally for people of pension age. It's worked out from household income (including benefits and pensions), your circumstances, any savings, and whether children or other adults live with you.

You will not qualify if you and your partner have over £16,000 in savings and investments — unless you receive the State Pension Guarantee Credit.

If awarded, the discount is credited to your account and you're sent a revised bill. It's set out in the council's Local Council Tax Support Policy 2026.

Second Adult Rebate

Available if you're liable for Council Tax, of State Pension credit age, and share your home with another adult (18+) who is on a low income and doesn't pay you rent — provided that person isn't your partner, isn't liable for the Council Tax themselves, isn't a joint owner or tenant, and isn't already getting a discount on your bill (for example a student discount).

If anything changes

Tell the council immediately — within 21 days — if someone moves in or out, someone in the house turns 18, your income changes, or your savings change. If you don't, and you've been overpaid, the amount will be deducted from your account and billed to you.

Appeals

If you disagree with a Council Tax Support decision, you can ask the council to explain it or look at it again — email benefits@gloucester.gov.uk, including your benefit claim number and the word "claim" in the subject line (for example, "Claim [your claim number]"). You can appeal to the Valuation Tribunal if the council still disagrees after reconsidering, or doesn't reply within two months.

Exemptions and premiums that depend on the property

Some properties are exempt from Council Tax altogether, whether occupied or not; others — long-term empty homes and second homes — now attract an extra premium on top of the standard bill. Tell the council within 21 days of anything changing, or you may get an amended bill and a penalty charge. Apply or notify by emailing revenues@gloucester.gov.uk with your Council Tax account reference in the subject line.

Empty properties

An unoccupied and unfurnished property gets a 25% discount for up to 6 months from when the furniture was removed — this attaches to the property, so it carries over even if it changes hands within that period. After 6 months, the full charge applies with no discount.

Empty Homes Premium

Since the premium is linked to the property rather than the person, buying or leasing a home that's already been empty and unfurnished doesn't reset the clock — you'll be liable for the extra charge if it stays empty. From 1 April 2025 (agreed at Full Council on 7 February 2024), the premium is:

  • +100% once empty 1 year or more (from 1 April 2025 — previously this tier started at 2 years)
  • +200% once empty 5 years or more (from 1 April 2020)
  • +300% once empty 10 years or more (from 1 April 2021)

Discount for major repairs or structural work

A property that needs, or is undergoing, major repair or structural alteration to make it habitable can get a 25% discount for up to 12 months.

Qualifying work
Major repairs to the roof structure (not re-shingling or replacing individual slates/tiles); rebuilding a defective chimney stack; rebuilding external walls, for example to extend the property; repair or renewal of foundations; replacing defective solid floors, floor joists or ceiling joists; totally gutting the property including removing most internal walls (not stud/partition walls); or making the property habitable again after a flood, fire or subsidence.
Work that does not qualify
General refurbishment; kitchen or bathroom replacement; general repairs; routine gas, electrical, heating or plumbing work (unless part of major works following a flood, fire or subsidence); re-wiring, timber or damp-proof treatment, re-plastering or draining works; health and safety concerns short of the property being structurally unsafe; short-duration works; and refurbishment between tenancies where no major works took place.

You can only claim this discount once per property (regardless of who was living there when it was previously awarded) — unless further major repairs are needed after a fire, flood or subsidence, since the discount belongs to the property, not the person.

If the property can no longer be considered a "dwelling" at all — because it needs major reconstruction, or substantial structural or major alterations to be habitable — you can ask the Valuation Office Agency to remove it from the Council Tax list. You must keep paying until you get official confirmation it's been removed, and contact the VOA again once the work is finished. VOA: gov.uk/council-tax-bands · ctinbox@voa.gov.uk · 03000 501 501 (England, Monday–Friday, 9am–4:30pm).

Second homes

From 1 April 2025, a 100% Council Tax Premium applies to "Second Homes" — properties that are substantially furnished but have no resident (no sole or main residence). This is on top of the standard Council Tax bill, and was approved at Full Council on 25 January 2024 under powers in the Levelling-up and Regeneration Act 2023 (which amends the Local Government Finance Act 1992).

You may be able to get a 50% discount if your second home is job-related. To apply, or if any of the exceptions above might apply to you, email revenues@gloucester.gov.uk with your Council Tax account reference in the subject line.

Annexes

Annexes get their own Council Tax band, and can qualify for the exemption or premium-exception classes referenced above (Classes T, W and F). Full detail on annexe-specific rules wasn't pulled into this reference — see Annexes on the council site.
