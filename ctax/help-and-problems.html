Help, scams & if you're struggling to pay

Council Tax is treated as a priority debt. If you're struggling, contact the council as soon as possible — the earlier you get in touch, the more options are open to you. This page also sets out exactly what happens if a bill goes unpaid.

Watch out for Council Tax scams

Fraudulent emails have circulated claiming you're in arrears and asking you to click a link to "check your payment status." Do not click the link — it can give criminals access to your computer or steal your banking details. The council will never ask for your bank details by email, or ask you to verify them through a link.

To genuinely check your Council Tax status, use your My Gloucester account, or contact the Council Tax team directly if you're at all unsure about a message you've received.

What happens if you don't pay

The council takes prompt action to recover unpaid Council Tax, because it funds essential local services. Here's the exact sequence:

  1. Reminder notice

    Sent if you miss an instalment. You have 7 days to bring your account up to date and must continue with your instalments. Recovery action doesn't stop if you only pay part of what's owed.

  2. Second reminder notice

    Sent if you miss a second instalment, again asking you to catch up within 7 days and continue paying. If you miss any further instalments, no more reminder notices are sent.

  3. Final notice

    Sent if you don't catch up within the 7 days, or miss another payment in the same financial year. You'll need to pay the full remaining balance for the year, or the council will take court action to recover the debt.

  4. Legal action — a Liability Order

    If you don't pay in full, or don't agree a payment arrangement, the council applies to the Magistrates' Court for a Liability Order — a demand for the full amount owed plus any costs incurred. Do not ignore a Liability Order; if you don't pay, the council will take further action.

  5. Further recovery powers

    Once a Liability Order is granted, the council can use any of the following:

    Enforcement agents (bailiffs)
    Instructed to recover the money owed, plus additional costs payable by you — they can take goods belonging to you to cover the debt.
    Deductions from earnings or benefits
    If you're working, your employer can be legally required to deduct money from your salary each month and pay it directly to the council — the amount depends on how much you earn, and continues until the debt is cleared. If you receive certain benefits, the council can ask the Department for Work and Pensions to deduct and pay it directly instead.
    Charging order
    Available once you owe more than £1,000. This secures the debt against your property, so it's claimed from any remaining equity once the property is sold and any mortgage repaid — the council may in some cases be able to insist on a sale.
    Bankruptcy
    Available once you owe more than £5,000. You'll be sent a statutory demand giving 21 days to pay in full; if you don't, a bankruptcy petition goes to court. If granted, you could lose your home if it's sold to pay the debt, your bank account(s) may be frozen, you may struggle to get credit, and the official receiver will investigate your affairs — and may control your spending for a considerable period if you're declared bankrupt.
    Committal to prison
    If enforcement agents can't recover the debt, the council can apply to the Magistrates' Court for a committal hearing — the first step towards a prison sentence of up to 90 days. If you receive a committal summons, contact the council immediately and seek legal advice. Paying the full amount outstanding means you won't need to attend court; if you don't pay and don't attend on the summons date, the court may issue a warrant for your arrest.
