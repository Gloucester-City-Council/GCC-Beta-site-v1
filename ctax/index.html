Understanding & paying your Council Tax

What Council Tax is, how much you'll pay this year, and every way you can pay it.

What Council Tax is

Council Tax is a charge collected by the council to help pay for local services — police, fire, rubbish and recycling collection, schools, roads and street lighting, libraries and planning. Most homes pay it; some are exempt, and some occupiers get a discount or reduction (see Money off your bill).

Who the money goes to

Gloucester City Council bills and collects Council Tax on behalf of four bodies:

  • Gloucester City Council (itself)
  • Gloucestershire County Council
  • The Police and Crime Commissioner for Gloucestershire
  • Quedgeley Town Council — only if your property is in the Quedgeley parish

From 2025/26, the Adult Social Care precept is no longer shown as a separate line — it's included within the main Gloucestershire County Council figure.

What it pays for, and this year's budget

Council Tax is the council's main source of locally raised income, used to close the gap between what it spends and what it receives from business rates and government grants. Government funding fell sharply between 2010 and 2024, and the council must balance affordability for residents against the risk of service cuts or larger increases later.

  • The average Council Tax for Gloucester City Council services (Band D) is £244.13 for 2026/27 — up £6.68 (about 3%) on the year before, under 13 pence a week.
  • Total net cost of council services: £22.446m (2026/27), up from £15.586m (2025/26).
  • Budget requirement: £17.199m (2026/27), up from £15.784m (2025/26).
  • Capital investment is funding major regeneration projects, including Kings Square and Kings Quarter, alongside continued transformation of council services.

Council Tax bands — and what determines your charge

To work out your bill you need to know three things: your property's valuation band, the discounts or exemptions that might apply, and whether you qualify for Council Tax Support. Every property in Gloucester sits in one of eight bands, set by the Valuation Office Agency (VOA) — not the council — based on the property's estimated value on 1 April 1991. Newer properties are still banded as if they had existed and been valued then.

Think your band is wrong?

Band appeals go to the Valuation Office Agency, not the council — contact the VOA to challenge your band. Keep paying your existing instalments while an appeal is being decided. You cannot appeal simply because you think the amount is too high.

Who's responsible for paying

Council Tax must be paid on every domestic property that isn't exempt. It's normally payable by whoever is aged 18 or over and has their sole or main residence at the property.

If more than one person lives there

The person liable is whoever sits nearest the top of this list — the hierarchy of liability:

  1. A resident freeholder (owner-occupier)
  2. A resident leaseholder
  3. A resident tenant
  4. A resident with a contractual licence to occupy (for example a caretaker or publican)
  5. Any other resident, including squatters or illegal tenants
  6. If no one lives there: the owner or tenant, if the tenancy hasn't ended

Joint and several liability applies — meaning two or more people can each be pursued for the whole amount owed — when: more than one person shares the same position in the hierarchy above, or residents are married, civil partners, or living together as such.

When the owner is liable instead of the resident

  • Residential care homes, nursing homes or hostels
  • Properties occupied by a religious community (monasteries, convents)
  • A minister of religion's residence, where their duties are carried out from it
  • Properties occupied by asylum seekers
  • Properties occupied by resident staff
  • Houses in Multiple Occupation (HMOs) — bedsits, or properties where more than one household pays rent for only the part they live in
  • Properties where every resident is under 18

Your bill, and appealing it

Your bill sets out what you owe, when, and how to pay — the council publishes a full guide, "Your Council Tax bill explained."

You can appeal your bill if you think: you're not the person who should be paying it, you haven't received a discount or reduction you were expecting, or you believe the property should be exempt.

You cannot appeal simply because you think your Council Tax bill is too expensive.

To appeal, write to the council explaining why. If you disagree with the final decision, or don't hear back within two calendar months, you can appeal to the Valuation Tribunal.

How to pay

Online
A 24-hour automated payment service. You'll need your Council Tax account number and a debit or credit card.
Direct Debit
The easiest way to keep on top of it — no need to remember monthly payments, and covered by the Direct Debit Guarantee. Choose to pay on the 1st, 15th, 22nd or 28th of the month. Around two-thirds of customers already pay this way. You'll need your account number and your bank or building society name, branch, sort code and account number — not all accounts accept Direct Debits, so check with your bank if unsure.
Phone
A 24-hour automated payment line: 0800 953 5588. You'll need your account number and card details.
Bank transfer or standing order
Account name: Gloucester City Council · Account number: 53093972 · Sort code: 20-20-23 · Payment reference: your Council Tax account number.
Post Office or PayPoint
Take your bill with you — it carries a barcode linked to your account.
Post
Send a cheque payable to "Gloucester City Council," with your account number written on the back, to: Gloucester City Council, Revenues and Benefits, Eastgate Management Suite, Eastgate Street, Gloucester, GL1 1PA.

Payment by Post Office, PayPoint or post may take longer to reach the council than online, phone, Direct Debit or bank transfer.
